**Short answer:** you can audit the basics of a website in 30 minutes with free tools. Check speed on mobile, whether Google can index your pages, whether the site is secure, and whether a visitor can find and use what matters. Here are ten checks, in the order I'd do them.

## Speed

1. **Run your homepage through [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/).** Look at the mobile result first, since most visits come from phones. Pay attention to the three Core Web Vitals: how fast the main content appears, how quickly the page responds to taps, and whether the layout jumps while loading.
2. **Find the heaviest files.** Oversized images are the most common cause of a slow site. If a photo is 3 MB and shows at 400 pixels wide, resize and compress it.

## Search visibility

3. **Search `site:yourdomain.com` on Google.** If few or none of your pages show up, Google isn't indexing them. [Why my website is not on Google](/en/blog/why-my-website-is-not-on-google/) explains the usual causes.
4. **Add the site to [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console).** It shows which pages are indexed, which queries bring visits and what errors Google found.
5. **Check titles and descriptions.** Each page needs its own title and description that say what the page is about. Duplicates or missing ones waste your best chance to attract a click.
6. **Check that you have a sitemap and one H1 per page.** A sitemap helps Google find every page, and a single clear H1 tells visitors and search engines what the page is about.

## Security

7. **Confirm the whole site uses HTTPS** and that `http://` redirects to `https://`. Then check the address on [Google Safe Browsing](https://transparencyreport.google.com/safe-browsing/search). More in [how to tell if a website is secure](/en/blog/is-your-website-secure/).
8. **Update everything.** Outdated plugins, themes and platforms are the most common way small sites get hacked. Remove what you don't use.

## Mobile experience

9. **Open your site on a real phone.** Can you read the text without zooming? Are buttons easy to tap? Does the contact option stand out?
10. **Test the main goal.** Try to do what a customer would: find a product, send a message, book. If it takes more than a few taps, that's a problem to fix.

## What to do with the results

Write down every problem and sort it by two things: how much it hurts and how long it takes to fix. Start with high impact and low effort, such as compressing images or fixing titles.
